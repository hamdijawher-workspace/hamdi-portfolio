Independent concept / Urban mobilityCivic UX / Mobility / Product design

Confidence while the city moves.

A civic mobility concept that turns route uncertainty into clear, timely decisions for urban commuters.

Independent product concept

01 / Commuter reality

The problem was not information.
It was confidence.

Public transport products often assume riders already understand the system. Transitun focused on the moments of doubt: whether the stop is correct, whether the transfer is ahead, and whether the rider can trust the next step without reconstructing the entire route.

The product had to show less, sequence it better, and make the next decision understandable while people were already moving.

02 / Product direction

Prioritize the
next good choice.

I set the product direction, structured the information architecture, and built a visual interaction language around stop awareness, route progress, transfers, tickets, subscriptions, and live tracking.

Cards, iconography, and hierarchy were tuned for fast recognition and reduced mental effort in noisy, imperfect transport conditions.

03 / Concept value

Clarity is a
civic responsibility.

Transitun became proof that route confidence can be designed rather than assumed. The interface reduces stress by surfacing the information riders need at the moment they need it.

When a product supports daily movement through a city, better UX affects confidence, independence, and how accessible the public system feels.
