Addinn Group / Smart Web PayUX research / Product strategy / Interface design

Trust at the point of payment.

Leading research, product strategy, and visual direction for a parking-payment journey built around clarity and trust.

Research-led product direction

01The challenge02Evidence to structure03Product principles04Direction05Research synthesis06Interaction decisions07Validation & next steps

01 / The challenge

A service problem,
not a screen problem.

Parking payment is time-sensitive. People are often mobile, already in transit, and unwilling to tolerate ambiguous cost, weak feedback, or technical instability.

The work connected two realities: make the current journey easier to trust and complete while defining a scalable foundation for booking, subscriptions, tracking, digital tickets, and smarter parking services.

At Addinn Group, I led the design work from stakeholder discovery and product definition through research, UX/UI direction, testing, presentation, and the visual system used to communicate the service.

02 / Evidence to structure

Make every design
decision defensible.

The program moved through six connected layers: business strategy, value innovation, user research, design goals, usability testing, and a design-system foundation.

Stakeholder interviews, three personas, journey mapping, reaction surveys, competitor review, and a detailed UX audit translated business ambition into priorities for the payment flow and broader service architecture.

03 / Product principles

Remove doubt at
every decision.

The research repeatedly pointed to the same requirement: make the next action visible before asking for commitment. That meant less friction at entry, more flexible search, transparent payment, and recovery states designed as part of the core journey.

01 / Entry

Guest mode and guided first use

Let occasional drivers pay without account friction, while a short tutorial explains the flow for first-time users.

02 / Search

Find parking your way

Support manual search, map exploration, and location scanning, with availability, price, duration, and reviews organized together.

03 / Payment

Clarity before “Pay now”

Show the remaining steps, simplify the summary, support more payment methods, and provide a usable receipt after the transaction.

04 / Recovery

Reliability is part of trust

Design loading, error, success, and retry states deliberately across mobile and desktop instead of treating them as exceptions.

04 / Direction

Clearer payment.
Stronger product story.

The resulting direction prioritizes shorter steps, stronger contrast, cleaner summaries, explicit transaction states, and predictable recovery. It also gives the team a stronger story for presenting Smart Web Pay as a service ecosystem across stakeholder, marketing, and communication contexts.

The core lesson is blunt: parking payment is never just a payment screen. Pricing, timing, availability, navigation, and technical reliability have to behave as one experience.

05 / Research synthesis

Turn evidence into priorities.

Start with the entire service

The 119-page research programme framed payment as part of a wider parking service. Stakeholder discovery established the business ambition; personas and journey mapping described different behaviours; the nine-product benchmark widened the set of patterns to consider. The useful output was a set of design priorities that could connect those perspectives.

Separate user needs from feature requests

Booking, subscriptions, digital tickets and tracking all compete for attention. I organised the experience around the immediate task: finding a suitable option, understanding the cost and completing payment. Broader service features need a clear place in the architecture without making an occasional driver learn the entire product.

Make the reasoning inspectable

The UX audit and research material informed entry, search, payment and recovery. Guest access addresses account friction; price and duration belong together because they describe one decision; a receipt closes the loop after payment. These links make the design direction easier to discuss than a collection of polished screens.

06 / Interaction decisions

Design the moments of commitment.

Before the transaction

The payment summary should let a driver check the parking location, duration and amount before proceeding. Keeping this information visible reduces the need to remember selections from an earlier screen. The primary action needs to describe what happens next, with supporting choices given less visual weight.

During uncertainty

A payment request can take time or fail. The design direction treats pending, error and success as distinct states. A pending message should discourage duplicate submissions; an error needs an actionable recovery path; a successful transaction needs a receipt the driver can find again. Visual polish alone cannot resolve ambiguous transaction status.

Across screen sizes

A responsive web-first approach makes the service accessible without requiring an app installation. It also introduces constraints: small screens, browser interruptions and inconsistent network quality. The same task hierarchy should survive those conditions, even when navigation and layout adapt to the available space.

07 / Validation & next steps

Measure whether doubt actually falls.

What this case demonstrates

The documented work covers research, product direction and interface principles. It does not establish a measured improvement in conversion or payment success. The public case therefore focuses on the decisions and deliverables rather than attributing business results to the design.

A focused next validation round

Ask occasional and regular drivers to find parking, explain the total cost, complete a payment and recover from an interrupted attempt. Observe hesitation, incorrect assumptions and whether they recognise the final transaction status. Test the full sequence, including recovery, rather than only asking whether the screens look clear.

Action points

  • Resolve issues that could cause incorrect payment or uncertainty about whether the transaction succeeded.
  • Test comprehension of the summary, navigation and receipt retrieval.
  • Define a baseline for completion, critical errors and support requests before release.
