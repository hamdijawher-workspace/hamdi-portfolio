Independent concept / Biometric registrationHardware UX / Accessibility / Research

Guided. Calm.
Accessible.

Designing a secure, intuitive biometric registration flow for first-time users at a standing self-service kiosk.

Product design / Service choreography

01 / Service risk

Trust starts before
the first touch.

Biometric kiosks sit at the intersection of service operations, trust, hardware, and physical behavior. Users are standing in formal, time-sensitive environments and may be uncertain about sensitive-data capture.

The interface has to coordinate a clear digital instruction with the correct physical action while keeping consent, accessibility, help, and recovery visible.

Journey6-stage registration
UsersFirst-time standing users
Accessibility44×44 px minimum targets
ScopeFace-capture MVP

02 / Architecture

Design the service
before the screens.

The work moved from persona and emotional journey to information architecture, accessibility requirements, wireframes, and polished interface states.

The six-stage journey covers approach; welcome, language, and accessibility; ID input and consent; guided face capture with live feedback; verification; then completion and exit. Help and recovery remain available throughout.

03 / Accessibility by default

Access is part of
the architecture.

Accessibility was specified as a system requirement rather than a final compliance pass. Visual, physical, audible, and tactile support were considered together for a public standing kiosk.

01 / Vision

AAA contrast intent

The visual system targets WCAG 2.1 Level AAA contrast with high-contrast and text-size controls available from the welcome state.

02 / Reach

44×44 px touch targets

Controls are sized for confident input and positioned within reach for standing users interacting with fixed hardware.

03 / Guidance

Voice and audio cues

Built-in spoken guidance and feedback help users understand each step without relying only on visual instructions.

04 / Tactility

Braille and haptic support

The concept includes braille numpad integration and haptic feedback for input and confirmation.

04 / Prototype foundation

Hardware UX is
service choreography.

The MVP turns the architecture into welcome, language, ID entry, face-scan, live-capture, confirmation, and completion states. Clear overlays and progress feedback make the machine feel supportive rather than opaque.

The design study moved from scenarios and desk research to wireframes and high-fidelity screens, establishing a concept foundation for later iris and fingerprint flows.
